# Ossuary — Music Prompts, Phosphor & Bone Version (one block per track)

This version is built from the title theme that already worked: **"a dungeon heard through a 1988 sound card, played in a cathedral that has been sealed for centuries."** Cold, patient machine music with something warm and human trapped underneath. Every track below uses the same two worlds, so the whole soundtrack feels like one game.

Three versions of the prompts now exist and share the same five-note motif, so tracks can be mixed freely:
- `music-prompts-phosphor.md` (this file): machine + bone hybrid, **the identity that was approved.**
- `music-prompts.md`: dark medieval, old instruments.
- `music-prompts-chamber.md`: intimate piano and cello chamber style.

## How to use

Each track is **one single prompt**. Copy the whole block inside the grey box and paste it into the generator's style/prompt box with **Instrumental ON**. Nothing else is needed. If a tool limits the length, cut sentences from the **bottom** of the block first, never from the top.

## The two worlds (what every block means)

**The Machine (left side of the stereo field, dry, small):**
- A bright, slightly metallic **FM bell** in the manner of an AdLib or OPL2 PC sound card. It carries the melody.
- A thin, nasal **12.5% pulse-wave arpeggio**, like a PC speaker that has been given a soul.
- A soft, round **triangle-wave bass** at low volume.
- **Noise percussion:** a high filtered tick like a knuckle on bone, a low noise thud like a footstep or a heartbeat. Never a drum kit.
- A faint **8-bit grain** and a little **tape wobble**, deliberately lo-fi. Sometimes a faint 60 Hz monitor hum.

**The Ossuary (right side of the stereo field, wet, huge):**
- A **pipe organ** with the reeds half closed, heard as a distant drone.
- A **solo cello**, close-miked, with audible bow noise and slow vibrato, placed dead centre as the human voice between the two worlds.
- **Found sounds:** knuckles on bone, an iron key turning in a lock, cloth dragged on stone, one slow water drip, wind, stone settling.
- **No voice of any kind.** Where a choir would be, use a held organ tone or a long cello note.

**Rules for every block:** never cheerful, never hopeful, never triumphant, never bright; no vocals, choir, humming or whispering; no orchestra or film-score swells; no drum kit, no guitar, no folk instruments; never more than four voices at once (five for the final boss only).

**The motif:** five notes, **D – F – E-flat – D – A** (shifted to other keys where noted). It is always recognisable and never played the same way twice. **The lie:** in a few tracks, exactly one E natural for exactly one beat, a bright and painful memory, before the E-flat returns.

## 01 · Title Screen — "Phosphor & Bone"

```
Instrumental only: absolutely no vocals, no voices, no choir, no humming, no whispering. Never cheerful, never hopeful, never triumphant, never bright: dark, sad, melancholic, funereal. The sound is a dungeon heard through a 1988 PC sound card, played inside a cathedral that has been sealed for centuries: cold, patient machine music, with something warm and human trapped underneath it. It is a funeral march played by a machine, never heroic and never an orchestral fantasy. Key D minor with the E-flat as the dread note, 6/4 time, 66 beats per minute, the pace of a hero descending stairs one at a time with no urgency and no turning back. About three minutes forty seconds, ending exactly as it begins so it loops with no seam. The machine layer, panned left, dry and small, is: a bright, slightly metallic FM bell in the manner of an AdLib sound card that carries the melody; a thin, nasal 12.5% pulse-wave arpeggio like a PC speaker given a soul; a soft, round triangle-wave bass at low volume; and noise percussion, a high filtered tick like a knuckle on bone and a low noise burst like a footstep. Everything is deliberately lo-fi, with a faint 8-bit grain and a little tape wobble. The organic layer, panned right and washed in a huge cold reverb with a seven-second tail, is: a pipe organ with the reeds half closed, a distant drone almost inaudible from the first bar, and, entering only in the second half, a solo cello, close-miked with audible bow noise, panned dead centre as the human voice between the two worlds. Found sounds: knuckles on bone, an iron key turning in a lock once, cloth dragged on stone, and one slow water drip on the downbeat of every eighth bar. The two layers never fully blend. The melody is a five-note motif: D, F, E-flat, D, then a leap up to A held for two beats, the first four notes one beat each. Structure. Bars 1 to 4, 0:00 to 0:22, the boot: silence, then a single low D on the triangle wave fades in like a monitor warming up, with a faint 60 hertz monitor hum under it; at bar 3 one FM bell strikes A and rings out alone; nothing else happens. Bars 5 to 12, 0:22 to 1:05, the descent: the FM bell plays the motif once per bar; beneath it the pulse-wave arpeggio falls D, A, F, D in steady eighth notes like footsteps on a spiral stair; a soft noise tick on beats one and four; the first water drip in bar 9. Bars 13 to 20, 1:05 to 1:50, the hall of bones: the organ swells on a D minor ninth chord, D, F, A, C, E; the bell harmonises the motif in parallel fifths, a medieval hollow sound; the arpeggio doubles in speed for a nervous shimmer; at bar 17 the bass drops to B-flat for four bars, the one moment of near-grandeur, which lasts only as long as the B-flat major seventh rings, and then the bass slides back down to A. Bars 21 to 28, 1:50 to 2:35, the cello and the lie: most of the machine drops out, leaving only the triangle bass and the bell; the solo cello takes the motif up an octave, longer and more ornamented, with slides and a vibrato that widens as it goes; at bar 25 the cello plays E natural instead of E-flat for exactly one beat, a bright, human memory of someone alive; at bar 26 the E-flat returns and everything is darker. Bars 29 to 36, 2:35 to 3:20, the seal: a slow, quiet crescendo; a steady heartbeat of low noise thuds on beats one and three with a faint half-volume pulse between them like a second heart; the organ holds an empty open fifth, D, A, D, with no third; the bell plays the motif four times and loses one note each time, five notes, then four, then three, then two, until only D and F remain. Bars 37 to 40, 3:20 to 3:40, the shutdown: everything stops except the monitor hum and a final bell on an open A that rings for twelve seconds while the hum fades over the last three; it ends on one single keyboard click, as if the machine went to sleep, so the loop feels like a reboot into bar one.
```

# Listening test for every generated track

Before accepting a take, check each of these. If any answer is wrong, reject it and regenerate.

1. **Vocals:** is there any voice, choir, humming, chant, whisper or "ah"? Reject.
2. **Mood:** does any part sound cheerful, hopeful, triumphant, bright or adventurous? Reject.
3. **Two worlds:** can you hear a cold, thin, lo-fi machine layer and a darker, wetter organic layer (organ, cello) kept apart? If it is all one texture, regenerate.
4. **Size:** more than four voices at once (five for the final boss)? Is there a drum kit, guitar or orchestra? Reject.
5. **Loop:** does the end match the start so it can repeat without a seam?
6. **Same game:** is the five-note motif recognisable, and does it feel like the title theme's world?
